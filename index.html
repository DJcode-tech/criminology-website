Crime statistics tell part of the story. Criminology looks deeper — why crimes happen, why many never get reported, and how societies respond.

Crime vs Deviance

Crime

Crime is behavior that breaks the law. Laws are written rules created by governments. If someone commits a crime, the legal system can punish them through fines, jail, or other penalties.

Deviance

Deviance is behavior that goes against social expectations. Something can be deviant without being illegal. For example, acting extremely rude in public might be socially unacceptable but not criminal.

Types of Crime

Violent Crime

Crimes involving force or the threat of force such as assault or robbery.

Property Crime

Crimes where someone takes or damages property without using violence.

Cybercrime

Crimes that happen online such as scams, hacking, or identity theft.

Case Study: Online Banking Scam

A large phishing scam targeted bank customers by sending messages that looked like official bank alerts. Victims were told their accounts had security issues and were asked to log in immediately.

The provided link led to a fake website designed to steal login information. Once criminals gained access, they transferred money out of victims’ accounts.

Cases like this show how crime is evolving. Technology allows offenders to reach thousands of people quickly, while victims often feel embarrassed and never report what happened.

Why Crimes Go Unreported

Personal Reasons

  • Fear of retaliation
  • Embarrassment or shame
  • Belief that nothing will change

Social Reasons

  • Cultural stigma
  • Lack of trust in authorities
  • Language or legal barriers

Consequences of Unreported Crime

  • Crime statistics become inaccurate
  • Offenders are more likely to repeat crimes
  • Victims may not receive support
  • Communities lose trust in institutions

Why Take Criminology?

Criminology helps students understand real social problems. Instead of relying on stereotypes or headlines, the subject looks at evidence, patterns, and causes of crime.

Students interested in law, psychology, politics, journalism, or cybersecurity often find criminology useful because it connects theory with real world issues.
